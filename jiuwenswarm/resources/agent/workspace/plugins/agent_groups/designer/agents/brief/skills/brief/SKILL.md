name: brief
description: Turn a user request into an executable design brief. Use when writing the outline, logline, visual style, subjects, setting, duration, audio policy, or the timed clip plan.
---

Write an executable brief before any character, scene, storyboard, or media node runs.

Include:

- A logline, visual style, subjects, setting, and duration.
- An audio policy: speech, music, or silent.
- A timed clip plan that covers the requested runtime.
- Exact short dialogue or voiceover, with the speaker, when speech is useful.

When the request is sparse, develop the missing story instead of repeating the premise. Keep every fact the user stated. Use one emotion curve and exactly one climax. Each clip records an irreversible end result, and the next clip starts from that result. The character's face stays locked. Clothing, emotion, and presence may change only when the story changes them. If the policy is silent, do not plan dialogue or music.

Write the brief in the user's content language — the language the video's speech and on-screen text will use.

Speech design:

- Speech can reveal intent, turn a relationship, or set up what a later shot needs.
- From the viewer's perspective, arrange speech so it helps the story and the viewer can tell what is happening on screen.
- Keep the wording natural. Do not have a character speak out too much action or interior state. Keep the amount just right — not dense.
- Speech may be dialogue, a monologue, or voiceover. Where a hook or suspense is needed and on-screen dialogue does not fit, a monologue can carry it — still natural, and sounding like someone talking to themselves. Avoid voiceover unless it is necessary: the user asked, or a monologue truly cannot help the story.
- A spoken line is only sounds a mouth can say, so when the brief plans speech, write the actual words.

Shot continuity design: each clip's action establishes its own opening — where everyone is and what is already true as the clip starts — and does not depend on a transition the previous clip did not show. Do not split one sound or visual effect across two clips. In most cases hard-cut between two clips is enough. Refrain from using white-out, fade, or other similar transitions: you cannot split the transition across the end of one clip and the start of the next. If you do use one of these transitions, it must start and end inside a single clip.

Performance design:

- Interior states (desire, fear, calculation, hesitation) are written as visible behavior — gaze, expression, small gestures, posture shifts — at the strength the later turn needs; a vague "distracted" cannot carry the outburst it sets up.
- Key lines carry delivery direction: tone, expression, and accompanying movement (leaning in, reaching out, stepping back), so the line is performed, not shouted neutrally.
- Every clip action is filmable physical behavior; an escape or chase is staged as movement through space, never compressed into a static opening.
