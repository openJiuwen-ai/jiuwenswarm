---
name: deck-planning
description: Read before building a new deck or restructuring an existing one, to work out the context, the story and the storyboard.
---

For a new deck the sequence is: understand the context, find the story, storyboard it, post the plan, and once the user agrees, build it one slide per mutation in the plan's order, verifying each slide as you go.

## 1. Understand the context

Know who is presenting to whom, what the audience needs to know or do afterwards, and the one conclusion the deck exists to deliver. Use what the conversation, the deck and the user's files already tell you; ask when the audience or the presenter's goal is not obvious. A board deck reads differently from an individual-contributor alignment call.

A live deck is narrated and can be lighter; a pre-read must carry the whole argument on its own. For a live deck built from scratch, find out the talk length or slide count; plan about 1.5 to 2 minutes per slide and leave room for questions when they are in scope.

## 2. Find the story

When the deck is partly built or the user has stated the story, keep their conclusions; if one looks false, say so in chat. When you are given source material or a broad research mandate, review it, form a few hypotheses, test them with the research and file tools available, and keep the ones that hold. The exploration does not go on slides. Reduce what holds to a big idea: what would you tell the audience if you had two minutes? State it to the user as a headline plus a short point of view before building.

## 3. Storyboard

For more than a slide or two, outline every slide's title and key message first.

- One message per slide. Two unrelated findings on one slide give the reader two problems instead of one.
- Someone reading only the titles should be able to follow the whole deck.
- The storyboard says what information each slide carries, not how it is designed; design comes later.
- A dependable structure is the pyramid: the upfront message, an executive summary previewing the sections, the sections, then a conclusion with next steps. Scale the structure to the deck: a short deck may need no intro or conclusion; a long one may need section dividers.
- Decide what the deck is for (a decision, consensus, a celebration, resolving a disagreement, opening a discussion) and build the storyboard to achieve it.
